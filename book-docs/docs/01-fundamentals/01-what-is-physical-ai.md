# What is Physical AI?

## Learning Objectives

After completing this chapter, learners will be able to:
- Define Physical AI and explain its relevance to modern robotics
- Distinguish between different types of AI systems (symbolic vs. learning-based)
- Understand the relationship between perception, planning, and action
- Identify real-world applications of Physical AI

## Lesson Content

### Section 1: Introduction to Physical AI

Physical AI represents the convergence of artificial intelligence with robotic systems that operate in the real world. Unlike traditional software-based AI that processes data in isolation, Physical AI systems must perceive their environment, make decisions, and execute actions in real-time.

**Key Concepts:**
- **Embodied Intelligence:** Knowledge emerges from interaction with the physical world
- **Closed-Loop Control:** Systems that sense, decide, and act continuously
- **Real-World Constraints:** Managing uncertainty, noise, and dynamics of physical systems

### Section 2: Core Components of Physical AI

Physical AI systems typically consist of three integrated components:

```
[Perception] → [Planning/Decision] → [Action]
    ↑                                    ↓
    └────────── Feedback Loop ──────────┘
```

**Perception:** Sensors gather information about the environment
**Planning:** Algorithms decide what action to take
**Action:** Actuators execute the planned movement

### Section 3: The Embodiment Thesis

The Embodiment Thesis suggests that intelligence cannot be separated from the body and its interaction with the environment. This has profound implications for how we design robots and AI systems.
